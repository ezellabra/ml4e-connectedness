\section{Conclusion}
Measured connectedness among emerging equity markets depends less on the estimation method than on whether the common factor is removed. At the weekly frequency connectedness is high, around 80\% for the dense measures, and almost entirely contemporaneous; the lasso VAR and Diebold--Yilmaz agree closely, and NETS reproduces the same pattern at a lower level. A single common factor explains more than half of the variance of returns, and removing it lowers connectedness by about 40\%, a share that is stable across windows, horizons and samples; the peak of 2020 and the decline afterwards reflect changes in the weight of this factor rather than in bilateral links. Most of the co-movement of emerging markets is therefore common exposure, not contagion. What cannot be claimed with the same confidence is the direction of spillovers between countries, which our data barely identify, and the structure of the idiosyncratic network, which is unstable in short windows. Natural extensions are to study volatility connectedness, as in the base paper, to use higher-frequency data or longer windows for the factor-adjusted estimators, and to add more markets, which would also improve the estimation of the common factor.
